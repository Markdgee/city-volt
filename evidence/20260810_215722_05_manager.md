# Executive Summary — SudSwitch / CityVolt Italy & Spain Launch

**Prepared by:** Priya, Manager
**For:** CityVolt Founders
**Date:** 2026-07-30

---

## 1. The Market Finding

Italy and Spain together represent 650,000+ annual new car buyers whose BEV adoption is stuck at 4–5% — roughly one-third the EU average of 14.5% — and unlike every other major EU market, neither country has shown meaningful acceleration since 2021, making them the largest underpenetrated volume opportunity in Western Europe for an affordable urban EV.

---

## 2. What We Built and Why It Matters

We built **SudSwitch**: a static, serverless web tool that takes five inputs from a prospective buyer (country, city, fuel spend, parking situation, daily km), fetches live Eurostat registration data at the moment of calculation, and returns a single personalised monthly cost delta — what they pay now versus what they'd pay with a CityVolt, with Italian *Incentivi Auto* or Spanish *MOVES III* subsidies applied. It routes outputs to two distinct lead buckets: test-drive intent (positive savings, viable charging) and infrastructure-waitlist intent (negative savings or street-only parking). This matters because it simultaneously generates qualified leads and produces a signal map of which cities need charging partnership deals before sales volume is achievable.

---

## 3. Recommended First Launch Market and Segment

**Italy first, specifically Milan and Rome, targeting 30–45 year-old urban professionals in aging small petrol hatchbacks.**

Italy's BEV share is not merely low — it is lower in 2023 than it was in 2021, a regression no other large EU market shows. That makes it the highest-contrast test case: if a well-scoped decision tool moves the needle here, the model is validated in the hardest possible conditions. The scrappage-linked *Incentivi Auto* subsidy (up to €13,500) is the single highest-leverage financial lever for this buyer profile, and the tool is already built to apply it correctly. Spain is the immediate second market — same tool, different subsidy path — but Italy's more extreme stagnation makes it the cleaner proof point.

Run a 30-day paid traffic test in Milan and Rome (Meta + Google, cost-sensitive urban commuter targeting) to establish lead cost and conversion rate for both the test-drive and waitlist buckets. The waitlist bucket is the more important outcome: high volume into the infrastructure-waitlist in a specific city is an actionable signal to prioritise charging partnership negotiations there before committing sales resources. Separately, assign Marlowe one focused question before the next planning cycle: does the Eastern European growth signal (PL, HU) represent a parallel opportunity worth a dedicated brief, or is it structurally different enough to deprioritise for CityVolt's current vehicle profile?
