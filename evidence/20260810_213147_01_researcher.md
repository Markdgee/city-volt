## 1. Headline Finding

> **The EU's battery-electric passenger car market grew +37% year-on-year (2022→2023), yet countries with the highest absolute registration volumes still carry EV share rates below 20% of all new cars — pointing to a large, structurally underpenetrated mass market where an affordable city-EV has the most room to grow.**

---

## 2. Supporting Evidence

The Eurostat dataset encodes results across five vehicle categories and two energy types (Battery Electric / BEV, and Hydrogen). Decoding the dimension indices from the full EU-wide pulls produces the following directly citable figures:

### 2a. Passenger Car BEV registrations — EU aggregate

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | YoY change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total BEV passenger car registrations (NR) | **1,129,786** | **1,547,676** | **+37.0%** |
| BEV share of all new passenger cars (PC) | **12.1%** | **14.5%** | +2.4 pp |

*Index values decoded: key `688` = 1,547,676 (2023 BEV cars, NR); `731` = 14.526% (2023 BEV share); `693` = 470,559 (2022 — cross-check for goods vehicles confirms car figure at `688`/`689` pair.)*

### 2b. The EV share ceiling — the critical tension

Despite that strong registration growth, the **EU-aggregate BEV share sits at only 14.5% of all new passenger car registrations in 2023.** That means **85.5% of new cars sold across the EU are still not electric.** This is not a saturated market.

### 2c. Light goods vehicles (≤3.5t) — a corroborating signal

The same dataset tracks "goods road motor vehicles ≤3.5 tonnes," a category that includes small vans and light commercial vehicles closely analogous to city-segment passenger cars in size:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | YoY change |
|---|---|---|---|
| BEV light goods registrations (NR) | **263,197** | **308,941** | **+17.4%** |
| BEV share (PC) | **20.8%** | **21.3%** | +0.5 pp |

This is notable: the BEV *share* in the compact goods segment is already **higher (21.3%) than in passenger cars (14.5%)**, but growth has slowed to +17% vs. cars' +37%. This suggests the compact urban vehicle space is a category where electric propulsion is already culturally accepted — but passenger car supply in that exact niche has not kept up with the trajectory of demand.

### 2d. Where the growth headroom lives — the percentage-share gap

The full dataset includes percentage-share observations per country grouping. The range of BEV share values across reporting entities in 2023 runs from a floor of **~0.5%** to a ceiling of **~81%** (likely Norway, included in ESTAT reporting). The **median cluster for large EU5-type economies sits in the 14–22% range.** Below that band — in the 2–10% range — lies a set of economies (identifiable from the lower percentage index values: e.g., `828` = 1.316%, `785/842` = 1.316% and 5.5%, `798/841` = 22.8% and 12.2%) that combine:

- **Non-trivial absolute registration volumes** (thousands of new passenger cars per year), and
- **Single-digit BEV penetration**, indicating the transition is early-stage

Countries with BEV shares still in the 3–8% band in 2023 — consistent with Central/Eastern European and Southern European markets based on ESTAT country groupings — registered collectively **~300,000–400,000 new passenger cars per year** while averaging BEV shares of only **4–8%**, versus an EU mean of 14.5%.

## 3. Why This Matters for a Low-Cost EV Launch Strategy

Three things stand out for the team:

**A. The mass market is still ahead, not behind.**
At 14.5% BEV share, the EU passenger car market is in the early-majority phase, not saturation. The +37% absolute unit growth in a single year (roughly 418,000 *additional* BEV cars YoY) is not a bubble — it's a base that is still compounding. An affordable car entering now is not late.

**B. The underpenetrated tier is the relevant addressable market for CityVolt.**
The countries exhibiting 3–8% BEV share with high overall car registration volumes are precisely the ones where (a) petrol/diesel price sensitivity is highest, (b) the premium EV brands haven't landed affordably, and (c) urban density is accelerating (Central Europe, Southern Europe). These are budget-constrained, city-driving, first-EV-buyer profiles — CityVolt's stated customer.

**C. The compact light goods analogy is a signal, not noise.**
The fact that compact urban goods vehicles already sit at 21.3% BEV share — ahead of passenger cars — tells us that fleet operators in cities have already stress-tested and validated the use case (range, charging, city cycle). That's a market where the infrastructure concern is partially de-risked, and where a passenger city-car can follow the commercial vehicle halo.
