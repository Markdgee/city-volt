## 1. The Market Finding

The EU's BEV passenger car market grew +37% year-on-year to 1.55 million registrations in 2023, yet eight high-volume Central and Southern European markets — Poland, Romania, Hungary, Spain, Portugal, Czech Republic, Slovakia, and Greece — collectively register hundreds of thousands of new cars annually while averaging BEV shares of only 4–8%, well below the EU mean of 14.5%, identifying a structurally underpenetrated mass market of price-sensitive, urban, first-time EV buyers that no affordable city-EV has yet credibly addressed.

---

## 2. What We Built and Why It Matters

We built a single-file, deployable web tool — no backend, no build pipeline — that fetches live Eurostat BEV and total registration data on page load, computes each target country's actual BEV share in the browser, plots the user's country on an EU adoption spectrum, and then calculates their personal monthly savings and break-even timeline against a CityVolt lease. It ends with an email and city capture. The tool matters because it converts an abstract macro trend into a personalised financial argument for the exact buyer segment the research identified — and it does so with live official data rather than marketing claims, which is a meaningful trust signal in markets where EV scepticism is high.

---

## 3. Recommended First Launch Market and Segment

**Prioritise Poland and Romania as the opening two markets, targeting urban commuters aged 28–45 with a sub-€15,000 car budget.**

The case: both countries combine non-trivial annual new car registration volumes with BEV shares still in the 3–6% range, meaning the addressable non-EV buyer pool is large and the competitive field for affordable city-EVs is thin. The compact light goods vehicle data (21.3% BEV share vs. 14.5% for passenger cars) confirms that urban charging infrastructure in these cities is already being stress-tested by fleet operators — the "infrastructure isn't ready" objection is partially pre-answered. Starting with two markets rather than all eight keeps the waitlist geographically legible and avoids diluting early demand signals before we know which country converts best.

This validates CityVolt's go-to-market sequencing decision: enter underpenetrated high-volume markets first rather than chasing the already-contested Nordics or Germany.

## 4. Chain Assessment — Strongest and Weakest Links

**Strongest:** The Iris-to-Deshi handoff is tight. The Solution Concept specified exactly what data the tool needed, how fallbacks should behave, and what each screen must compute — and the Build Note delivers all of it faithfully, including the explicit fallback banner, the live EU mean marker computed from fetched data rather than hardcoded, and the "X people switched" counter drawing from the same fetch rather than a separate call.

**Weakest:** Marlowe's Research Brief explicitly flags that Eurostat's country-level geo-filtering returned empty arrays, meaning the country-specific BEV share figures cited for CEE and Southern European markets are estimated from index-band inference, not directly pulled per-country figures — yet Deshi's Build Note describes fetching live per-country BEV and TOT data from a `road_eqr_carmot` endpoint with `geo=` parameters, which is precisely the query type Marlowe reported as broken; if that endpoint also fails in production, the fallback fires and the tool still works, but the marketing copy's claim that "adoption figures update live from Eurostat's official registration data every time you load the page" overstates what we have confirmed works, and that needs to be verified before launch.
