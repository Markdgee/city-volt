# Solution Concept

---

## 1. Concept Name

**SudSwitch — The Southern Europe EV Readiness & Savings Calculator**

---

## 2. One-Paragraph Pitch

Italy and Spain together represent 650,000+ new car buyers per year who are choosing ICE vehicles at roughly 3× the rate of the EU average — not because they don't want EVs, but because no one has shown them, concretely, what switching *actually costs them* in their city, with their commute, under their country's specific incentive regime. SudSwitch is a decision-support tool that takes a prospective buyer's real situation (city, current car, annual km, parking type, electricity tariff band) and produces a single, personalized "switch or wait" output: the monthly cost delta between keeping their current car and buying a CityVolt, accounting for fuel savings, Italian *Incentivi Auto* or Spanish *MOVES III* subsidies, and local charging infrastructure density. It exists *because* the IT/ES market is stuck at 4–5% BEV share while the EU average is 14.5% — the gap is not a preference gap, it's an information and confidence gap that a well-scoped tool can close.

---

## 3. Who It's For & The Decision It Helps Them Make

**Persona:** Giulia or Marcos — a 30–45 year-old urban professional living in Milan, Rome, Madrid, or Barcelona. They commute 8,000–14,000 km/year in a 7–12 year-old small petrol hatchback (Fiat Panda, SEAT Ibiza tier). They are cost-sensitive, not ideology-driven. They have *considered* an EV but stalled because they can't easily calculate whether it pencils out for them personally, they're unsure about charging logistics in a dense urban apartment block, and they distrust headline subsidy numbers that feel abstract.

**The decision the tool helps them make:** "Should I replace my current car with a CityVolt *right now*, or is this not yet the right move for my situation?" — answered with a number (monthly €), a confidence signal (green/amber/red), and a clear next step.

---

## 4. Core Features to Build

- **5-question intake form (Screen 1)**
  - Country selector: Italy / Spain (gates the subsidy logic)
  - City or postal code (used to pull charging point density category: dense / moderate / sparse)
  - Current car fuel type + rough annual fuel spend in € (slider: €50–€300/month)
  - Parking situation (private garage with socket / street only / workplace charging available)
  - Typical daily km range (slider: under 30 / 30–60 / 60–100 / over 100)

- **Savings breakdown card (Screen 2 — primary output)**
  - Side-by-side monthly cost: "Your current car" vs "CityVolt"
  - Line items shown explicitly: fuel vs electricity cost, maintenance delta (fixed estimate), subsidy applied as amortised monthly reduction over 4-year ownership
  - **Italy path:** applies *Incentivi Auto 2024* tiered amounts (up to €13,500 if scrapping EURO 0–5); **Spain path:** applies *MOVES III* (up to €7,000 with scrappage)
  - Net monthly delta shown as a large, single headline number: e.g., "You'd save €87/month"
  - Charging readiness indicator: based on parking input — green (home charging viable), amber (workplace or semi-public), red (street-only, flags this as a real constraint with suggested workaround)

- **"What's holding the gap" context strip (Screen 2 — secondary)**
  - One data callout anchored to Marlowe's finding: "Only 4.2% of new cars sold in Italy in 2023 were electric — vs 14.5% EU average. Most of that gap is buyers like you who haven't yet seen the numbers."
  - Not marketing copy — framed as a factual orientation so the user understands they are an early-majority, not a fringe buyer

- **Single CTA (Screen 2 — bottom)**
  - If delta is positive (saves money) + charging is green/amber: "Reserve a CityVolt test drive in [city]" → lead capture
  - If charging is red or delta is negative: "Get on the waitlist for home charging partnerships in [city]" → separate lead bucket, signals to CityVolt which cities need infrastructure deals before sales are viable

- **Shareable result card (optional, low-build-cost)**
  - "My CityVolt number: –€87/month" — one-tap share, drives organic loop among cost-conscious peer groups

## 5. How It Ties Back to Marlowe's Finding

Marlowe's data identified a precise, quantified anomaly: Italy's BEV share is **4.2% in 2023, lower than its own 2021 level of 4.6%**, and Spain's is **5.4% — flat for two years** — while the EU average accelerated to **14.5%**. The combined market is 650,000+ annual units. This is not a market that has decided against EVs; it is a market that has not yet received a credible, localized economic case.

Every element of SudSwitch is load-bearing *because of that specific finding*:

- The **country split** (IT vs ES) exists because the subsidy regimes differ and Marlowe's data shows both markets are lagging by similar magnitudes for potentially different structural reasons (Italy's share actually *fell*, Spain merely stalled)
- The **scrappage logic** is foregrounded because both *Incentivi Auto* and *MOVES III* are scrappage-linked — and older small petrol hatchbacks dominate the parc in both countries, making this the single highest-leverage subsidy lever for the CityVolt target buyer
- The **charging readiness flag** directly addresses the infrastructure constraint that explains why IT/ES lag even as Poland and Hungary (with equally sparse infrastructure) are growing fast — the difference is that Eastern European buyers are purchasing more in purpose-built EV-accessible housing; Italian/Spanish buyers are concentrated in dense pre-war apartment stock
- The **"only 4.2%" context strip** turns Marlowe's headline finding into a psychological reframe: this tool only makes sense to build *because* that gap
